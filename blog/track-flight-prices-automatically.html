How to track flight prices automatically (without checking every day)

FlyAtlast · September 2026

You've been there: same trip, fifth refresh of the day, and the price moved while you were making coffee. Checking by hand is a losing game, and it's the one part of trip planning a tracker can take off your plate.

1. Decide your budget first

Pick a total you'd happily pay for the whole trip, for everyone travelling. That number turns a wall of moving fares into a yes-or-no question. If you don't have one yet, look at a few fares once, then set a figure slightly under what feels normal.

2. Track the exact trip

Use the route and dates you'd actually book. If your dates have some give, allow a few days either side. It widens what a tracker can find without changing your trip.

3. Choose a tracker that shows the real itinerary

An alert that just says "prices dropped" isn't much use. You want the flight numbers, times, stops and fare type, so you know what the price is for. FlyAtlast shows these in the app.

4. Act when it fits, not on every wiggle

Small moves are noise. When a real fare comes in under your budget, decide then. If you aren't ready to book, a short price watch can keep an eye on a fare you have already found.

5. Know what trackers can't do

No tracker holds a fare or a seat, and none can promise where prices go next. Treat an alert as a prompt to check and book, not a guarantee.

Do it once, set a number you're happy with, and let the checking happen without you.
