Why do flight prices change so often?

FlyAtlast · September 2026

The same flight can cost one amount in the morning and another by evening. It feels random, but there are a few ordinary reasons behind it.

One seat, several fare levels

Airlines do not sell a flight at a single price. They sell seats in groups, each with its own fare and conditions, and open or close those groups as a flight fills or as departure gets closer.

Demand and timing

Busy dates, holidays and popular routes tend to cost more. Fares can shift as a flight fills, or when demand is lower than expected.

Competition and sales

Airlines watch each other. A fare on one carrier can move when another matches or undercuts it, or when a sale starts or ends.

Small moves and real moves

Many changes are tiny, a few dollars up and back down, and mean very little. Real drops are rarer, and they do not announce themselves.

What that means for you

Nobody can reliably time these moves for you. The practical answer is to know your budget and be ready when a real fare fits it, which is what an automatic tracker is for.
